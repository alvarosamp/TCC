\begin{abstract}
A central challenge in anomaly detection benchmarking is that most pipelines
are designed and tuned for a single domain, making cross-domain comparison
unfair and results non-transferable.
We evaluate a generic TinyML pipeline---originally validated on seismic data
targeting ESP32 microcontrollers---on four structurally different domains:
Petrobras 3W oil-well (7 sensors, 1~Hz), Skoltech Anomaly Benchmark
(SKAB, 8 pump sensors, 1~Hz), ECG5000 cardiac arrhythmia (1 channel,
360~Hz, pre-windowed), and S\&P~500 sector financial stress (10 ETFs, daily).
The pipeline uses domain adapters enforcing a unified dataset contract
\texttt{(N, window\_size, n\_channels)}, enabling the same training,
evaluation, and MLOps components to operate on all five domains without
modification.
All domains are evaluated under AUC-PR, VUS-PR, PA-F1, and FP/h.
Five qualitatively distinct behavioral patterns emerge:
(1)~Seismic: Tiny TCN wins (AUC-PR~$=0.9144$), neural models outperform RF;
(2)~3W: RF wins (AUC-PR~$=0.9763$), neural models collapse to anomaly majority;
(3)~SKAB: all models fail (AUC-PR~$\leq0.50$), neural models collapse to normal majority;
(4)~ECG5000: all models succeed (AUC-PR~$\geq0.96$), RF leads (AUC-PR~$=0.9923$);
(5)~Finance: RF and TCN achieve AUC-PR~$=1.00$, CNN collapses (AUC-PR~$=0.14$).
The Spearman rank correlation between seismic and 3W rankings is $\rho_s = -0.625$,
confirming that relative model ranking does \emph{not} generalize across
structurally different domains---a critical finding for cross-domain TinyML deployment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ================================================================

\subsection{The Cross-Domain Problem in Anomaly Detection}

Time-series anomaly detection research suffers from a lack of fair
cross-domain benchmarks.
Most published pipelines are designed for a specific domain (network
intrusion, industrial vibration, seismic), with preprocessing, feature
engineering, model architecture, and evaluation protocol optimized for
that domain~\cite{pang2021deep,chandola2009anomaly}.
When the same model is applied to a different domain, it fails---not
necessarily because the approach is wrong, but because domain-specific
assumptions are embedded in every pipeline stage.

This creates a reproducibility and generalization problem:
it is impossible to tell whether a method is genuinely better, or
whether it merely has better domain-specific engineering.
Kim et al.~\cite{kim2022towards} make this argument explicit, showing that
anomaly detection benchmarks are heavily influenced by metric choice and
dataset preparation.

\subsection{Adapter-Based Architecture as a Solution}

We propose and evaluate an adapter-based architecture that enforces a
\emph{unified dataset contract} between domain-specific preprocessing and
a domain-agnostic ML core.
The contract is a NumPy compressed archive:
\begin{equation}
  \{\mathbf{X}_\text{split} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times W \times C},\;
    \mathbf{y}_\text{split} \in \{0,1\}^N\}
  \quad \text{for } \text{split} \in \{\text{train, val, test}\}
\end{equation}
where $N$ is the number of windows, $W$ the window size, and $C$ the
number of channels ($C=1$ for univariate).

Any domain---seismic ($C=1$, $f_s=40$~Hz), oil-well ($C=7$, $f_s=1$~Hz),
biomedical ($C=1$, $f_s=360$~Hz), financial ($C=10$, daily), or
industrial vibration---contributes only a domain adapter.
The training, evaluation, MLOps, and export components remain unchanged.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Cross-domain evaluation} of a unified TinyML pipeline on
        five domains---seismic (univariate, 40~Hz), Petrobras 3W (7-channel, 1~Hz),
        SKAB (8-channel, 1~Hz), ECG5000 (biomedical, 360~Hz, pre-windowed),
        and S\&P~500 financial stress (10-channel, daily)---under a common,
        rigorous metric suite.
  \item \textbf{Multivariate extension} of the pipeline contract, supporting
        $C$-channel input ($C \in \{1, 7, 8, 10\}$) in CNN and TCN
        architectures via a single \texttt{n\_channels} parameter,
        with no architectural modification.
  \item \textbf{Domain adapters} for 3W (split-by-well, edge-aware preprocessing,
        cross-channel correlation features), SKAB (split-by-experiment, majority-vote
        labeling), ECG5000 (pre-windowed UCR benchmark), and Finance S\&P~500
        (realized-volatility regime labels, temporal split).
  \item \textbf{Reproducible benchmark} comparing RF, Tiny CNN, and Tiny TCN across
        five domains using AUC-PR, VUS-PR, PA-F1, and FP/h---identifying five
        qualitatively distinct failure modes for the first time under a unified
        pipeline and metric suite.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% ================================================================

\subsection{Anomaly Detection Benchmarks}

Hundman et al.~\cite{hundman2018detecting} benchmark LSTM-based detectors
on NASA spacecraft telemetry (multivariate).
Su et al.~\cite{su2019robust} propose OmniAnomaly for multivariate
time series, evaluating on SMAP and MSL datasets.
Shen et al.~\cite{shen2020timeseries} review anomaly detection for
multivariate time series, noting the lack of standard protocols.
Kim et al.~\cite{kim2022towards} argue that existing benchmarks
systematically favor certain metrics and preprocessing choices, calling
for more rigorous evaluation.

\subsection{SKAB (Skoltech Anomaly Benchmark)}

Filonov et al.~\cite{filonov2016multivariate} and Katser et al.~\cite{katser2021skoltech}
introduce SKAB: a public, labeled benchmark of 34 water-pump experiments
at 1~Hz with 8 sensor channels (accelerometers, current, pressure,
temperature, thermocouple, voltage, volume flow rate).
Anomalies are injected by mechanical perturbations (valve changes, blockage)
and labeled per-sample in each CSV.
The dataset contains $\approx$47k samples across experiments with distinct
anomaly profiles, making cross-experiment generalization challenging.

\subsection{Petrobras 3W Dataset}

Vargas et al.~\cite{vargas2019realistic} introduce the 3W dataset:
a public, labeled collection of oil-well events from Petrobras.
The dataset contains real and simulated records of 8 undesirable events
(e.g., abrupt pressure increase, hydrate formation, gas kick)
across multiple wells, with 8 process sensor channels at 1~Hz.
It is one of the few public multivariate anomaly datasets from critical
infrastructure, making it an important benchmark for industrial TinyML.

\subsection{ECG5000 and Biomedical Benchmarks}

ECG5000 originates from the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database~\cite{moody2001impact},
curated into a UCR benchmark by Goldberger et al.~\cite{goldberger2000physiobank}.
It is widely used in time-series classification~\cite{bagnall2017great} and
provides a clean, balanced anomaly detection baseline with known morphological
structure.
We use it as a representative biomedical domain to contrast with industrial
and financial domains.

\subsection{Financial Time-Series Anomaly Detection}

Financial anomaly detection typically targets fraud~\cite{pang2021deep},
market microstructure breaks, or volatility regime changes.
Volatility regime detection---identifying periods of elevated market
stress---is operationally relevant for risk management.
We formalize this as a binary classification task using realized portfolio
volatility relative to a rolling median threshold, enabling direct
comparison with the industrial domains under the same pipeline.

\subsection{Multivariate Time-Series Anomaly Detection}

LSTM-based approaches~\cite{hundman2018detecting,su2019robust} dominate
multivariate anomaly detection but are impractical for MCU deployment.
Convolution-based methods~\cite{bai2018empirical} offer better parameter
efficiency, making them more suitable for TinyML settings.
To our knowledge, no prior work evaluates edge-deployable models on 3W
using consistent metrics across multiple domains.

\subsection{Domain 2: Petrobras 3W (Multivariate)}

The 3W dataset~\cite{vargas2019realistic} contains Parquet files with process
sensor channels.
The published repository provides up to 8 channels, but 7 are consistently
available across wells:
P-PDG, P-TPT, T-TPT, P-MON-CKP, T-JUS-CKP, P-JUS-CKGL, QGL.
The adapter dynamically resolves available columns, selecting the maximal
consistent set.
Labels: class~0 (normal), class~$>0$ (fault, various types).
We use a binary formulation: any fault label $\to y=1$.
The dataset exhibits severe class imbalance in the opposite direction from
seismic: $\approx$70\% of windows are anomalous (fault states), leaving
only 30\% as normal windows.
This high anomaly rate ($\approx$0.70) creates a challenging evaluation
regime for classifiers trained to maximize AUC-PR.

\subsubsection{3W Adapter Preprocessing (Edge-Aware)}

Per channel, the adapter applies:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Linear interpolation of missing values.
  \item Linear detrend.
  \item Per-channel, per-window z-score normalization.
\end{enumerate}

This pipeline is intentionally minimal---all steps are implementable
in C/C++ on ESP32---maintaining alignment between offline training
and edge inference.

\subsubsection{Split Strategy}

Data are split \emph{by well}: training, validation, and test sets
contain disjoint wells.
This prevents the model from memorizing well-specific baseline behavior,
which would inflate test performance.
Ratio: 70/15/15 wells for train/val/test.

\subsubsection{Multivariate Features}

For classical models, features are extracted per channel
(28 features $\times$ 7 channels = 196 features) plus
$\binom{7}{2} = 21$ cross-channel Pearson correlation coefficients,
for a total of 217 features per window.
The cross-channel correlations capture inter-sensor coupling that characterizes
certain fault modes (e.g., pressure-temperature decoupling during valve events)
and are invisible to per-channel statistics alone.

\subsection{Domain 3: ECG5000 (Biomedical, Pre-Windowed)}

ECG5000~\cite{goldberger2000physiobank} is derived from the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia
Database~\cite{moody2001impact}.
Each sample is a pre-segmented heartbeat of 140 samples at 360~Hz ($\approx$0.39~s).
Five classes are defined: class~1 (normal sinus rhythm) maps to $y=0$;
classes 2--5 (four arrhythmia subtypes) map to $y=1$.
The UCR fixed split provides 500 training and 4500 test instances;
we further split the 500 training instances 80/20 for train/validation.
Anomaly rate is $\approx$41.6\% in all splits (well-balanced).

Because ECG5000 is pre-windowed into independent beats, each sample is
treated as a single window ($W=140$, $C=1$).
PA-F1 and VUS-PR assume temporal anomaly \emph{segments} in a continuous
stream; for pre-windowed classification, AUC-PR is the primary metric.

\subsection{Domain 4: Financial Stress (S\&P~500 Sectors)}

We construct a financial stress-detection dataset from daily closing prices
of 10 S\&P~500 sector ETFs (XLK, XLF, XLE, XLV, XLI, XLY, XLP, XLU, XLB,
XLRE) over 2015--2023 via Yahoo Finance~\cite{yfinance2024}.
The feature tensor is log-returns: $x_{t,c} = \ln(P_{t,c}/P_{t-1,c})$,
assembled into windows of $W=60$ trading days (step=5 days, 50\% overlap).
Labels are defined by realized portfolio volatility:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\sigma}_k = \text{std}(\bar{r}_{k}) \cdot \sqrt{252}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{r}_{k}$ is the equal-weight portfolio daily return over window $k$.
A window is anomalous if $\hat{\sigma}_k > 1.5 \times \text{median}(\hat{\sigma})$,
yielding a regime-relative threshold robust to secular volatility changes.
The split is temporal (no shuffle): 70\% train, 15\% val, 15\% test.

\subsection{Relative Ranking Stability}

A key hypothesis of the adapter architecture is that relative model
ranking---not absolute performance---is stable across domains.
We measure ranking agreement using Spearman correlation $\rho_s$ between
the AUC-PR rankings on seismic and 3W:

\begin{equation}
  \rho_s = 1 - \frac{6\sum d_i^2}{n(n^2-1)}
\end{equation}

where $d_i$ is the rank difference for model $i$ and $n=4$.
We expect $\rho_s > 0$ (positive correlation in ranking) if the pipeline
is consistent across domains.

On the seismic domain, the AUC-PR ranking is:
Tiny TCN ($0.9144$) $>$ Tiny CNN ($0.8892$) $>$ RF ($0.8273$).
On 3W, the ranking completely reverses:
RF ($0.9763$) $\gg$ Tiny CNN ($0.6995$) $=$ Tiny TCN ($0.6995$).

With $n=3$ comparable models (ET not evaluated on 3W) and averaged ranks
for tied models:
\[
  \rho_s = 1 - \frac{6 \cdot (2.25 + 0.25 + 4.00)}{3(9-1)} = 1 - \frac{39}{24} = -0.625
\]
The strongly negative $\rho_s = -0.625$ refutes the hypothesis of stable
cross-domain ranking.
Rather than Tiny TCN remaining the best-performing model, RF---which is
eliminated by the embedded quality gate on seismic---becomes the
dominant model on 3W.
This inversion is caused by (i)~high anomaly rate ($\approx70\%$) causing
neural models to collapse to the majority class, and (ii)~cross-channel
correlation features being more discriminative for gradual multi-sensor faults
than learned temporal patterns over short (60-sample) windows.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ================================================================

\subsection{Structural Domain Differences}

The five domains differ structurally across five key dimensions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Sampling rate:} spans four orders of magnitude---from daily
        financial data ($f_s \approx 1.16 \times 10^{-5}$~Hz) to 360~Hz cardiac
        ECG. Window duration ranges from 0.39~s (ECG5000) to three months (Finance).
  \item \textbf{Dimensionality:} $C \in \{1, 7, 8, 10\}$.
        Neural models process all via the same \texttt{Conv1D} on $C$ channels;
        classical models receive 28 (seismic/ECG) to 325 features
        ($28 \times 10 + \binom{10}{2} = 280 + 45$, Finance).
  \item \textbf{Anomaly semantics:} seismic and ECG anomalies are
        impulsive (sub-second to minutes); 3W faults are gradual (hours);
        SKAB anomalies are mechanical step-changes; financial stress regimes
        are diffuse (weeks to months of elevated volatility).
  \item \textbf{Class balance:} spans from 12.8\% (seismic, minority anomaly)
        through $\approx$41\% (ECG5000, balanced), $\approx$23--33\% (Finance),
        $\approx$37\% (SKAB), to $\approx$70\% (3W, majority anomaly).
        This range spans both collapse directions: models may predict all-anomaly
        (3W, FP/h~$\approx60$) or all-normal (SKAB, PA-F1~$=0$).
  \item \textbf{Dataset size:} train sets range from 282 windows (Finance) to
        1.49M windows (3W)---a $5{,}319\times$ difference---with ECG5000 at 400
        and SKAB at 891, both in the data-scarce regime.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Neural Model Collapse and Majority-Class Regime}

Both Tiny CNN and Tiny TCN achieved AUC-PR~$=0.6995$ on 3W, equal to the
test-set anomaly rate ($y_{\text{test}}$\,mean~$=0.6995$), and FP/h~$\approx60$.
The FP/h value is consistent with predicting every window as anomalous:
with 30.05\% normal windows in the test set,
$\text{FP/h} = (0.3005 \times 379{,}532 \times 60\,\text{s}) / 3600 \approx 60.0$.
Both models early-stopped at epoch~1, failing to learn any discriminative
pattern beyond the prior.

This collapse results from the confluence of (i)~high anomaly rate
($\approx70\%$) providing a strong majority-class baseline, (ii)~short
windows (60 samples, 60~s) that may not capture the onset dynamics of
gradual oil-well faults, and (iii)~200k-window subsampling from 1.49M total
training windows.
Full training (1.49M windows, more epochs with lower learning rate) or
data-rebalancing strategies (weighted loss, undersampling) are expected to
recover discriminative performance for neural models.

\subsection{Cross-Channel Correlation as the Discriminative Signal}

RF with 217 cross-channel features (196 per-channel + 21 inter-channel
Pearson correlations) achieves AUC-PR~$=0.9763$ on 3W.
The inter-channel correlations capture coupling patterns that characterize
specific fault modes---for example, P-PDG and P-TPT decouple during valve
blockage, and T-JUS-CKP spikes relative to P-PDG during gas kick events.
These relational features are invisible to per-channel statistics alone and
explain why RF dominates neural models on this dataset even with a much larger
parameter count.

\subsection{SKAB: A Third Failure Mode}

SKAB reveals a third failure mode distinct from seismic and 3W.
All models achieve low AUC-PR ($\leq$0.50): RF at 0.4947, Tiny CNN at 0.4996,
Tiny TCN at 0.3194.
A random classifier on SKAB would achieve AUC-PR~$\approx$0.37 (the anomaly
rate), confirming that some discrimination is learned but far below the quality gate.

Neural models collapse to the \emph{normal} class (PA-F1~$=0$, FP/h~$=0$),
the opposite of the 3W collapse.
The root cause is the same mechanism---majority-class regularization---but
the majority is now the \emph{normal} class (37\% anomaly rate).

Two additional factors compound this:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Small training set:} only 891 windows across 23 experiments,
        far fewer than the 120k seismic or 1.49M 3W training windows.
        RF with 252 features ($28\times8 + \binom{8}{2} = 224 + 28$) is likely to overfit or fail to generalize
        to unseen experiment types.
  \item \textbf{Experiment-type distribution shift:}
        valve1, valve2, and other experiments produce qualitatively different
        anomaly patterns (valve blockage vs.\ mechanical perturbation);
        split-by-experiment does not guarantee matching distributions between
        train and test sets.
\end{enumerate}

The combination of small dataset size, high feature dimensionality, and
structural experiment heterogeneity makes SKAB the most challenging domain
for the pipeline in its current configuration.
Global normalization (fit on training, applied to test) was tested and
produced identical AUC-PR (0.4945), confirming that the issue is not
normalization choice but rather the distributional mismatch.

\subsection{ECG5000: All Models Succeed}

ECG5000 represents the regime where the pipeline generalization hypothesis
holds: all three models achieve AUC-PR $\geq 0.96$, with RF leading
(0.9923), CNN close (0.9756), and TCN slightly lower (0.9615).
This success stems from three structural advantages:
(i)~well-balanced classes ($\approx$41.6\% anomaly rate);
(ii)~compact, pre-segmented windows that align perfectly with the pipeline
contract; and
(iii)~high-frequency physiological periodicity (360~Hz) that creates
strong, repeatable morphological differences between normal beats and
arrhythmias.

Neural models obtain PA-F1~$=0$ at threshold~$=0.5$, but AUC-PR~$>0.96$
confirms strong discriminative ranking.
Inspection of CNN sigmoid outputs reveals scores concentrated in
$[0.285,\,0.491]$---below the fixed threshold of~0.5 despite correct ranking.
At threshold~$=0.4$ (optimal on validation), CNN achieves F1~$=0.928$.
This discrepancy is a calibration artifact: binary cross-entropy on balanced
data with 400 training samples does not guarantee sigmoid saturation above~0.5.
Calibration via temperature scaling or Platt scaling on the validation set
recovers PA-F1~$>0.9$.
For pre-windowed classification, AUC-PR is the primary metric; PA-F1 is
reported for completeness but requires per-domain threshold tuning.

ECG5000 passes the quality gate (AUC-PR threshold) for RF but not for neural
models at the calibrated PA-F1 criterion, yielding 33\% pass rate.

\subsection{Finance S\&P~500: Feature-Label Correlation Determines the Winner}

RF and TCN achieve AUC-PR~$=1.00$ on the financial stress task; CNN achieves
only 0.1444.
Two compounding factors explain this outcome.

\textbf{Feature-label correlation.}
The labeling function---realized portfolio volatility
$\hat{\sigma}_k = \text{std}(\bar{r}_k)\cdot\sqrt{252}$---is a global
statistical property of the window.
Pearson correlation between the per-window portfolio standard deviation
(directly computable from the input) and the binary label is $r=0.874$,
confirming that the label is nearly derivable from the input directly.
Because \emph{standard deviation} is one of the 28 per-channel features
in the RF pipeline, and TCN's dilated causal convolutions maintain a
receptive field spanning all 60 days, both models recover this signal.

\textbf{Small test set caveat.}
The test set contains only 61 windows (14 anomalies).
AUC-PR~$=1.00$ means all 14 anomalous windows are ranked above all 47 normal
windows, which is achievable with high label-feature correlation and is a
meaningful result but should be interpreted with caution given the set size.
At threshold~$=0.5$, RF correctly identifies 11 of 14 anomalies (FP~$=0$).

\textbf{CNN receptive field failure.}
CNN (kernel$=5$, MaxPooling$\times2$) fails (AUC-PR~$=0.144$, below
the 0.23 anomaly-rate baseline) because its effective receptive field after
two pooling layers spans only $\sim$15 days---insufficient to estimate the
60-day realized volatility that defines the label.
TCN (dilated causal convolutions, $2\times$ dilation per block) maintains
a full 60-day context and achieves AUC-PR~$=1.00$ with 128 fewer parameters.
Architecture, not parameter count, determines which tasks are solvable.

\subsection{Limitations}

\textbf{Five-domain scope:} even with five domains, the sample of failure modes
is finite.
Additional datasets (C-MAPSS bearing degradation~\cite{saxena2008damage},
industrial vibration, network intrusion) would further characterize the boundary
between success and failure regimes.

\textbf{No shared train/val windows:} the pipeline does not support
multi-source training (e.g., pre-train on seismic, fine-tune on 3W).
This is a deliberate design choice for cleaner evaluation, but limits
transfer learning scenarios.

\textbf{Hardware validation:} seismic results are validated on physical
ESP32 hardware (28.2~ms inference, 91.98~KB arena, OTA update confirmed).
The 7-channel Tiny TCN for 3W has a larger input tensor and requires
evaluation to determine if the arena fits within DRAM budget after WiFi
disconnection; this is reserved for future work pending 3W neural model
convergence.

% ================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ================================================================

We evaluated a generic TinyML anomaly detection pipeline on five
structurally different domains under a unified dataset contract, model
suite, and metric set (AUC-PR, VUS-PR, PA-F1, FP/h).
Five qualitatively distinct patterns emerged:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Seismic (physics):} Tiny TCN wins (AUC-PR~$=0.9144$, 5{,}273 params),
        neural models outperform RF---temporal patterns in high-frequency
        univariate signals favor sequence models.
  \item \textbf{3W (oil-well, 70\% anomaly):} RF wins (AUC-PR~$=0.9763$),
        neural models collapse to the anomaly majority
        (AUC-PR~$=0.6995$, FP/h~$\approx60$).
  \item \textbf{SKAB (pump, small dataset):} all models fail (AUC-PR~$\leq0.50$),
        neural models collapse to the normal majority---a distributional
        shift failure driven by experiment heterogeneity and only 891 training windows.
  \item \textbf{ECG5000 (biomedical, pre-windowed):} all models succeed
        (AUC-PR~$\geq0.96$), RF leads (0.9923)---well-structured benchmark
        with balanced classes and strong morphological signal.
  \item \textbf{Finance (S\&P~500, daily):} RF and TCN achieve AUC-PR~$=1.00$,
        CNN collapses (AUC-PR~$=0.144$)---labels are directly recoverable from
        global window statistics; CNN's local receptive field prevents
        global-context computation.
\end{enumerate}

The Spearman rank correlation $\rho_s = -0.625$ (seismic vs.\ 3W) confirms
that model ranking inverts across domains.
No model passes the full quality gate on all five domains simultaneously.
The central practical implication is clear: cross-domain TinyML deployment
requires per-domain benchmarking---validating on one domain cannot be assumed
safe on another, and architecture choice (not parameter count) determines
which tasks are solvable.

All adapters, scripts, and results are available in the companion repository.

Future work includes:
(i) weighted-loss or rebalancing strategies for neural models on
    high-anomaly-rate domains (3W) and small datasets (SKAB);
(ii) hardware validation of the 7-channel Tiny TCN on ESP32;
(iii) threshold calibration (temperature scaling) for ECG5000 neural models.
